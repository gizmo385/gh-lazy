# B32: Toasts lose their bottom rows when the message wraps at the drawn width only

- Id: B32
- Status: open
- Python reference: `textual/widgets/_toast.py` `ToastRack` (`overflow-y: scroll`, auto height), Textual 8.2.5; reached by every `notify(` call in `ui/`
- Recommendation: Keep the bottom padding; don't copy the clipping.
- Rows: `main-layout.toasts`, `toasts.view-maximized`

`ToastRack` is `height: auto`, docked to the bottom with `overflow-y: scroll`, so it keeps a hidden 2-column scrollbar
gutter. Textual sizes the rack's height from each toast's height at `max-width: 50%` of the full screen width
(`W / 2`). It then lays the toasts out inside the gutter, at 50% of `W - 2`. When a message (or title) needs more
lines at the narrower width, the toasts are taller than the rack. The excess lines are summed across the whole stack and
cut off the rack's bottom. That bottom is the newest toast.

- One excess line hides the newest toast's bottom padding row. Example: the maximize toast at 100×30. Its message is
  47 characters, which fits the 47 content columns of `W / 2 = 50` but wraps at the 46 columns of the drawn width
  `(100 - 2) / 2 = 49`. The parity capture `maximize` `max` at 100×30 shows no padding row under "return.".
- Two or more excess lines also hide message text. Checked headless with Textual 8.2.5 at 100×30
  (`run_test(notifications=True)`):
  - With two maximize toasts, the newest shows "Current view maximized. Press escape to" and loses "return." and its
    padding. The older one is complete.
  - With one toast whose message is 47 `x`, a space and 47 `y`, the toast loses the second `y` chunk and its padding.
  - With the stack `[A, maximize, C]`, the maximize toast in the middle is drawn in full (rows 20-24). The newest toast,
    `C`, loses its bottom padding: its region is `y=26 h=4`, and row 29 is clipped.
- At widths of 122 columns and more, both widths are the full 60 columns and nothing is lost.

The Rust toasts are sized and drawn at the same width (`min(60, (W - 2) / 2)`), so they keep every row. At 100×30 the
Rust maximize toast is therefore one row taller than Python's, and starts one row higher. Found while implementing #79
([PR #133](https://github.com/gizmo385/gh-lazy/pull/133)). Until Chris answers, the recommendation stands.
